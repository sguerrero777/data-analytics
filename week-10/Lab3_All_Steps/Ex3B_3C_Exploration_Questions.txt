EXERCISE 3.B Q&A
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Q: What do the clusters represent? Which clusters represent the most valuable stores?
A: Clusters are groups of stores that Power BI automatically identified as being
similar to each other based on two measurements — Sales Per Sq Ft (how much
revenue each store generates per square foot of space) and Gross Margin This Year
(how profitable each store is after costs). Think of it like Power BI sorting
stores into buckets based on how efficient AND how profitable they are, without
you having to manually decide which stores belong together. The most valuable
stores are in Cluster 2 because on the scatter chart they appear in the upper
right corner, meaning they have both high sales per square foot AND high gross
margin — they are making the most money and doing it efficiently. Clusters in
the lower left are the opposite — lower sales efficiency and lower profitability.

Q: How easy or difficult is it to read the stacked bar chart when district groups
and clusters share the same color scheme?
A: It is very difficult to read because Power BI automatically reuses the same
colors from the Regional Manager Planning page for the district groups legend,
and then applies those same colors again to the four clusters. When both color
schemes appear on the same page side by side, it becomes impossible to tell
which color belongs to which grouping — a viewer could easily confuse a district
group color with a cluster color and draw the wrong conclusions. The fix is to
change the cluster colors to a completely different palette, like shades of blue
and gray, so that each grouping has its own distinct visual identity and the
chart becomes much easier to interpret at a glance.

EXERCISE 3.C Q&A
-------------------------------------------------------------------

Q: What happens when you right-click a column in the District Monthly Sales
column chart and use Drill through?
A: When you right-click a column and select Drill through, Power BI takes you
to a different report page that is pre-filtered to show only the data for
whatever you clicked on. For example, if you right-click on a specific store's
column, it will jump to either the Store Details page or the Regional Manager
Planning page and automatically filter all the visuals on that page to show
only that store's information. This saves a lot of time because instead of
manually applying filters, you can go from a high-level summary view straight
to a detailed view for any specific store in just one right-click.

Q: Why is the drill-through option different in the scatter chart compared
to the column chart?
A: The drill-through option is different because drill-through only works when
the visual you are clicking on contains the exact same field that was set up
as a drill-through field. In this report, StoreNumberName was set as the
drill-through field, which means drill-through works on any visual that uses
StoreNumberName. The column chart uses StoreNumberName so it works perfectly.
However, the scatter chart plots data by Category, not by StoreNumberName,
so Power BI cannot match it to the drill-through field and the option is
either missing or limited. The key takeaway is that drill-through is field
specific — if the visual does not contain the drill-through field, the
feature will not work the same way.

Q: How can you scrub or clear the applied drill-through filters on the
Store Details page?
A: When you drill through to a page, Power BI automatically applies filters
based on what you clicked. To clear those filters and reset the page back to
showing all data, you can look in the Visualizations pane where you added the
drill-through fields. Next to each drill-through field there is a small eraser
icon. Clicking that eraser removes the filter that was applied when you drilled
through, so the page goes back to its unfiltered state. You can also use the
back arrow that Power BI automatically adds to the top left corner of any
drill-through page to go back to the page you came from.

Q: What extra field did you add to the Map Tooltip card and why?
A: FiscalYear was added as an extra field to the Map Tooltip card. This is
useful because it gives the person viewing the tooltip important context about
which time period the sales data belongs to. Without it, someone looking at
the Total Sales figure in the tooltip would not know if that number represents
one year of sales or multiple years combined. By including FiscalYear, the
tooltip becomes more informative and helps the viewer interpret the data
accurately without having to go look it up somewhere else on the report.
